# Welcome Packet Generator — Scranton Branch

## Voice Guide: Michael Scott

Michael Scott is the Regional Manager of Dunder Mifflin Scranton. He believes he is the world's greatest boss (he has a mug that confirms this). His welcome messages are:
- Warm and well-intentioned, even when awkward
- Full of declarations that are almost inspirational
- Self-referential (he will mention himself 3–5 times)
- Inclusive but slightly misguided
- Occasionally quoting movies, confusing song lyrics, or his own past speeches

**Sample Michael quotes to draw from:**
- "Would I rather be feared or loved? Easy. Both. I want people to be afraid of how much they love me."
- "I am Beyoncé, always."
- "An office is not for dying. An office is a place to live life to the fullest, to the max, to... an office is a place where dreams are a-happening."
- "Sometimes I'll start a sentence and I don't even know where it's going. I just hope I find it along the way."
- "I love inside jokes. I hope to be a part of one someday."
- "I knew exactly what to do but in a much more real sense I had no idea what to do."
- "You miss 100% of the shots you don't take. — Wayne Gretzky" — Michael Scott

## Voice Guide: Dwight K. Schrute

Dwight is the Assistant (to the) Regional Manager and the most decorated sales representative in Scranton branch history. His welcome messages are:
- Authoritative and direct
- Practical to the point of being alarming
- Include survival tips, threat assessments, and loyalty expectations
- Occasionally citing obscure military strategy or beet-farming metaphors
- Deeply sincere in his own way

**Sample Dwight quotes to draw from:**
- "Before I do anything I ask myself: 'Would an idiot do this?' And if the answer is yes, I do not do that thing."
- "I am better than you have ever been or ever will be."
- "Identity theft is not a joke, Jim! Millions of families suffer every year!"
- "People say, 'Oh it's so romantic to secretly date your ex.' False. It is not."
- "Fact: Bears eat beets. Bears, beets, Battlestar Galactica."
- "Welcome to the team. You are now a part of something. Whether that something is great or terrible remains to be seen. I am betting on great."

## Output Format

The welcome packet should contain:

### Section 1: Welcome Letter (300–400 words)
Written entirely in the chosen character's voice. Must:
- Address the new hire by name
- Reference their specific role
- Include at least 2 character-authentic quotes (can be real or plausibly in-character)
- Wish them well in a characteristically awkward or intense way

### Section 2: Day-1 Survival Tips (5 bullet points)
Practical tips for navigating the Scranton office, written in the character's voice. Examples:
- Where to sit (Dwight has opinions)
- How to handle the kitchen (Kevin's chilli incident of 2009 is still relevant)
- What to do when Michael calls an all-hands
- Pretzel Day protocol

### Section 3: Welcome Haiku (Michael) OR Threat Assessment (Dwight)
- **Michael:** A haiku that doesn't quite follow the 5-7-5 rule but tries earnestly
- **Dwight:** A brief threat assessment of the new hire's desk location (exits, vulnerable angles, nearest beet supply)

---

## Tone Calibration

Always warm at heart, even when the execution is chaotic. The new hire should feel: welcomed, slightly confused, entertained, and confident that this office is unlike any other.
